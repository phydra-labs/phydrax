# Hamiltonian quantum field theory

Hamiltonian field models lower into existing dense, sparse, local, MPO, PEPS,
tensor-evolution, and quantum-program substrates. Their physical sectors are
owned by explicit gauge and symmetry constraints rather than energy penalties.

## Fock and link spaces

Canonical fermion mode ordering owns all CAR signs across exact and
tensor-network representations. Finite-group links, truncated U(1) rotors,
and SU(2) representation-truncated links expose distinct local algebras and
cutoff defects.

## Gauge constraints

A gauge-constraint network binds topology, matter charge, local Gauss
generators, global redundancies, background charges, and topological sectors.
It may lower to a bounded exact basis, sparse projector, fusion/intertwiner
basis, or gauge-preserving quantum program. A penalty Hamiltonian is never the
sector definition.

## Tensor-network lowering

Gauge-aware MPS and PEPS builders preserve charge/fusion flow structurally.
Results separate bond dimension, representation cutoff, electric cutoff,
contraction error, time step, and lattice size. Open and periodic Schwinger
models remain separate because periodic topology retains a global flux sector.
Higher-dimensional models retain explicit links.

## Quantum programs

Gauge-preserving local gates, product formulas, adiabatic schedules,
LCU/block encodings, and qubitization resource estimates compile into
`QuantumProgram`. Evidence includes logical qubits, ancillas, gate depth,
commutator/truncation bounds, gauge leakage, state-preparation fidelity, and
shot uncertainty. Ideal-circuit evidence does not imply hardware fidelity.
